Along Manchester Avenue in The Grove, a St. Louis neighborhood known for its eclectic mix of bars, boutiques, and creative businesses, Ember & Ice Recovery has become an unexpected anchor of the area's evolving identity. What opened two years ago as a modest single-room sauna studio has since expanded into a full recovery suite offering infrared heat, cold plunge tubs, and compression therapy under one roof.

"We started small because we honestly weren't sure how much demand there'd be in St. Louis for this kind of dedicated recovery space," said co-founder Talon Reyes, who launched the studio with business partner Simone Okada after both left careers in corporate wellness consulting. "We were wrong about the demand. We've had to expand twice."

## Contrast Therapy Takes Hold

The studio's most popular offering has become its guided contrast therapy sessions, which alternate short intervals in an infrared sauna with brief immersions in a chilled plunge tub, a practice with roots in Scandinavian wellness traditions that has gained mainstream traction through fitness and recovery communities online.

"People come in skeptical about the cold plunge specifically," Okada said. "But once they get through that first minute, the reaction is almost universal. There's a rush afterward that keeps people coming back, and there's a growing body of interest in the potential circulation and recovery benefits, even though we're always careful to be clear this isn't a substitute for medical care."

Ember & Ice has built partnerships with several fitness studios and running clubs in the surrounding Forest Park Southeast and Botanical Heights neighborhoods, offering discounted recovery packages to members as a complement to their regular training.

## An Unexpected Client Base

While the studio initially anticipated a client base dominated by serious athletes, Reyes said one of the more surprising developments has been the volume of remote workers and desk-bound professionals who now make up a significant share of daily visits, drawn less by athletic recovery and more by stress relief and a structured break from screen time.

"We have people who come in on their lunch break just to sit in the sauna for twenty minutes with their phone locked in a locker," Reyes said. "That wasn't necessarily the client we pictured when we designed this space, but it makes sense. Recovery isn't only physical."

The Grove's identity as a walkable, mixed-use neighborhood has helped, Reyes said, with many clients arriving on foot from nearby apartments or offices rather than driving in specifically for an appointment.

## Balancing Growth With Quality

As demand has grown, Reyes and Okada have been cautious about scaling too quickly, adding staff and equipment incrementally rather than expanding into a second location right away. The studio now employs four recovery specialists trained to guide clients through contrast protocols safely, including screening new clients for cardiovascular conditions that might make cold immersion inadvisable without medical clearance.

"Safety has to come first with this kind of service," Okada said. "We're not interested in growing so fast that we cut corners on how carefully we onboard new clients."

## What's Next for Ember & Ice

The studio is currently piloting a membership model aimed at giving regular clients unlimited monthly access, a shift from its original pay-per-visit structure, and is exploring whether The Grove's continued growth as a neighborhood might eventually support a second, smaller Ember & Ice location elsewhere in the city.

"This neighborhood has been the right home for us," Reyes said. "Whatever comes next, we want to make sure we're still delivering the kind of experience that made people trust us in the first place."
