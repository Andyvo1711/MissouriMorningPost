The recovery studio boom that transformed fitness culture in larger cities has finally found solid footing in Columbia, where Tiger Line Recovery has spent the past two years building a client base that spans University of Missouri student athletes, recreational runners, and office workers looking for relief from long hours at a desk.

Housed in a converted warehouse space near downtown Columbia, the studio offers a menu built around infrared sauna sessions, compression boot therapy, and percussive massage, along with guided cooldown classes that combine light stretching with breathwork.

## Filling a Niche Near Campus

Studio founder Jamal Kirkwood, a former college track athlete who studied exercise science at Mizzou, said he opened Tiger Line after noticing a gap between what campus athletic facilities could offer student athletes and what was available to the general public nearby.

"Athletic department facilities are obviously excellent, but they're not accessible to the average person in Columbia who's training for a half marathon or just trying to recover from sitting at a desk all day," Kirkwood said. "I wanted to bring some of that same recovery science to everyone else."

The studio's proximity to campus has made it a popular spot for graduate students and walk-on athletes who don't have access to varsity-level facilities, as well as Columbia residents unaffiliated with the university entirely.

## Infrared Heat and the Science of Recovery

Tiff Bosley, Tiger Line's lead recovery specialist, said the infrared sauna has become the studio's most requested single service, particularly among clients managing chronic muscle tightness or training through a heavy competition season. Unlike traditional saunas that heat the surrounding air, infrared units warm the body directly, which Bosley said allows for a more tolerable, sustained session at a lower ambient temperature.

"We get a lot of runners in the winter especially," Bosley said. "It helps with circulation, and honestly a lot of people just need the mental reset of twenty-five minutes with no phone, no noise, just heat."

The studio pairs sauna sessions with compression therapy, where clients wear inflatable boots that apply rhythmic pressure to the legs, a treatment increasingly common in professional and collegiate training rooms that Tiger Line has helped bring to a broader Columbia audience.

## A Growing Local Following

Columbia resident Dana Whitcomb, a marathoner who trains year-round, said she now visits Tiger Line twice a week during peak training blocks. "Before this opened, the closest thing to real recovery equipment was driving to Kansas City or St. Louis," Whitcomb said. "Having it here has genuinely changed how quickly I bounce back between hard training days."

## Expanding the Model

Kirkwood said Tiger Line is in the process of adding a cold plunge feature to complement its heat-based offerings, part of a broader trend in recovery studios pairing contrast therapy, alternating heat and cold exposure, which some research suggests may enhance circulation and reduce perceived muscle soreness.

He's also exploring partnerships with local running clubs and youth sports organizations to offer discounted group sessions, hoping to extend the studio's reach beyond its current core of university-affiliated clients.

"Columbia's a town that takes its athletics seriously, at every level, not just Division I," Kirkwood said. "We want Tiger Line to be a resource for anyone in this town who's pushing their body and needs somewhere to help it recover."
