The University of Missouri broke ground Wednesday, Sept. 2, on the Payne Energy Innovation Center in Columbia, a research facility focused on making technology that uses power and water more efficient and sustainable.

## A record gift for a college

The building is named for David Payne, an MU graduate and chair of St. Louis electrical contracting firm PayneCrest Electric. Payne donated $50 million to the MU College of Engineering, which the university calls the largest single investment in a school or college in its history.

The University of Missouri System Board of Curators approved the project in June 2025. The groundbreaking was one of several events the university held that day, which also included a Power the Roar campaign milestone update and a ribbon cutting for the North End Zone renovation at Memorial Stadium.

## What will be built

The 116,000-square-foot center is expected to open north of Lafferre Hall in July 2028. According to the Missouri News Network, it will include a shared instrumentation suite, communal networking and mentorship spaces, shared student offices, lab support space, a custom lab and a data lab.

Payne said he hopes the center becomes a place where research pushes boundaries and big ideas are explored. Marisa Chrysochoou, dean of the College of Engineering, pointed to the growing energy demands of artificial intelligence, saying cooling data centers is becoming a higher priority. UM System President Mun Choi said the center will bring together expertise from many disciplines to take on major energy problems.

## State funding uncertain

The General Assembly allocated $2 million for the center in the fiscal year 2027 budget, but Gov. Mike Kehoe froze that funding. The governor can reverse the freeze if state revenue allows.
