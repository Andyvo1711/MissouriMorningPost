A customer who rarely writes negative reviews shared feedback about a recent visit to Maria Nails & Spa. They began by saying they understand how challenging it is to run a small business, and that "honest feedback is the only way businesses can improve." They also arrived with a clear idea of what they wanted and even took photos of the result, which makes their account a constructive source of learning for any team.

## Shape and Length Requests

The customer said they specifically asked for short, square nails, but the finished shape was uneven and did not look like what they had requested. Square nails depend on straight edges and matching corners, so small differences are easy to see. A quick look at the nails from the customer's point of view, with all fingers side by side, can show whether the shape is consistent.

A few habits help. Technicians can confirm the shape and length with a short question or a reference photo before filing begins, and file each nail to match the others rather than finishing one at a time. Checking the nails from above and from the side before moving to polish helps catch uneven corners early, while they are still easy to adjust.

## Polish Application and Finish

The customer also felt the polish was applied unevenly, with inconsistent coverage and a finish that did not look polished. They noted they expected better quality for the $60 they paid.

A clean polish application relies on steady preparation and thin, even coats. Preparing the nail surface, keeping the polish off the skin, and covering each nail edge to edge give a smoother result. A final inspection in good light, with the customer, allows touch-ups before they leave the chair. If a customer feels the finish is uneven, an immediate correction is far easier than a return visit.

## Cleanliness at the Station

What concerned the customer most was seeing a used bandage wrapper left on their chair. They said this made them question the salon's hygiene standards. Even when an item is small and harmless, a customer may take it as a sign of how thoroughly the station was cleaned.

Resetting a station between clients can help. Clearing every item from the chair, table, and floor, wiping surfaces, and replacing towels and liners give the next client a clear sign that the space is fresh. A final look around the station before a new customer sits down takes only seconds, and it shows care for the customer's comfort and confidence.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers several lessons. First, confirm the nail shape and length before filing begins, and check that all nails match before moving to polish. Second, make a final inspection of polish coverage and finish with the customer so any touch-ups happen in the salon. Third, reset every station completely between clients, so that nothing from the previous visit remains in view. A customer who takes the time to explain a concern kindly is giving the salon useful guidance, and acting on it helps turn feedback into stronger service.
