In a converted historic home a few blocks from the Independence town square, Silver Creek Wellness has spent the past three years building a practice that treats acupuncture not as an alternative curiosity but as one piece of a broader, coordinated approach to client health.

Licensed acupuncturist and clinic founder Wren Adebayo opened Silver Creek after nearly a decade practicing in Kansas City, drawn to Independence by both lower overhead costs and what she described as an underserved local demand for holistic care options outside the greater Kansas City core.

## One Roof, Multiple Disciplines

Unlike many standalone acupuncture practices, Silver Creek employs a small team that includes a licensed nutrition counselor and a stress-management coach who also leads guided breathwork sessions, allowing clients to combine services in a single visit or across a coordinated multi-week program.

"So much of what we see is connected," Adebayo said. "A client comes in for chronic tension headaches, and it turns out sleep, diet, and stress are all tangled up in that. If I can only offer needles, I'm only addressing one piece of a much bigger picture."

The clinic's most popular offering is a six-week "Reset Program" that combines weekly acupuncture sessions with two nutrition consultations and a series of guided stress-reduction exercises, designed for clients managing chronic conditions like migraines, insomnia, or digestive issues that haven't fully resolved through conventional treatment alone.

## Building Trust in a Skeptical Market

Adebayo acknowledged that acupuncture still faces skepticism from some prospective clients in the Independence area, and said much of her early outreach involved simply explaining what a session actually involves and what conditions the practice is realistically suited to address.

"I'm very upfront that this isn't a replacement for a client's physician," Adebayo said. "We work alongside primary care, not instead of it. A lot of our referrals now actually come from local doctors who've seen it help patients with pain management or stress-related symptoms."

Family physician Todd Kessler, who has referred several patients to Silver Creek over the past two years, said he's grown more comfortable recommending the clinic as a complementary option. "I'm careful about who I refer patients to, but Wren's practice is well-run and she communicates clearly about what to expect," Kessler said. "For patients who've tried the standard options for chronic tension or stress-related symptoms without full relief, it's a reasonable next step."

## A Growing Client Base

Nutrition counselor Ines Calloway, who joined the clinic last year, said Independence clients have responded well to the collaborative model, particularly older adults managing multiple overlapping health concerns. "We get a lot of clients in their fifties and sixties who are tired of feeling like they're bouncing between specialists with nobody looking at the full picture," Calloway said. "Here, we're actually talking to each other about a shared client's care."

## Looking Ahead

Adebayo said Silver Creek is planning to add a part-time massage therapist to the practice later this year, further rounding out its integrative model. She's also developing a community workshop series on stress management aimed at introducing local residents to holistic practices in a low-pressure setting.

"A lot of people just need an entry point that doesn't feel intimidating," Adebayo said. "Once they see how it fits into their actual life, that's when it clicks."
