Rosalind Kettner-Duval took over Tri-State Builders Guild in 2013 during one of the most difficult periods in the company's history, inheriting a construction firm with a damaged safety record and a client list that had shrunk after a series of project delays and a serious jobsite injury the previous year. Twelve years later, the Joplin-based contractor has become one of the region's most sought-after builders for commercial and institutional projects, a turnaround Kettner-Duval attributes almost entirely to rebuilding the company's safety culture first and its reputation second.

"You don't fix a reputation problem with a marketing campaign," Kettner-Duval said at a job site where her crews were finishing a commercial building near downtown Joplin. "You fix it by making sure nobody gets hurt on your watch for years in a row, and eventually people notice."

## Rebuilding From the Ground Up

Kettner-Duval's first major decision as owner was to halt new project bidding for nearly four months while she overhauled the company's safety protocols, brought in outside safety consultants, and retrained every crew member on updated jobsite procedures. The pause cost the company revenue it could barely afford at the time, a decision several board members and longtime clients questioned.

"I had people tell me I was going to run the company into the ground by turning down work," she said. "But taking on projects with the same safety culture that caused our problems in the first place would have run us into the ground faster, just less visibly."

The investment eventually paid off. Tri-State Builders Guild has gone more than a decade without a serious jobsite incident, a record Kettner-Duval now features prominently in bids for institutional clients like school districts and healthcare systems that weigh safety records heavily in contractor selection.

## Rebuilding the Joplin Skyline

The company played a significant role in commercial rebuilding efforts across Joplin in the years following the region's tornado recovery period, work that Kettner-Duval said shaped the company's identity as a builder focused on durability and community trust rather than the lowest possible bid. Tri-State Builders Guild has since completed projects ranging from medical offices to a regional distribution warehouse.

"People in this town remember which contractors showed up and did the work right during recovery, and which ones cut corners," Kettner-Duval said. "We made sure to be in the first group."

## Growing a Skilled Workforce in a Tight Labor Market

Tri-State Builders Guild now employs 88 people, and Kettner-Duval has invested heavily in apprenticeship partnerships with regional trade schools to address a persistent shortage of skilled tradespeople in southwest Missouri's construction industry. The company covers a significant portion of apprenticeship costs for employees pursuing electrical and HVAC certifications, a benefit Kettner-Duval said has improved retention substantially.

The company is currently bidding on a multi-building institutional project that would be its largest contract to date. "Ten years ago, nobody would have handed us a project this size," Kettner-Duval said. "Now it's a normal Tuesday phone call."
