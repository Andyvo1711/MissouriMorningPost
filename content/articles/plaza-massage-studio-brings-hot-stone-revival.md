For a while, hot stone massage seemed to have fallen out of favor, edged out by trendier offerings like percussive therapy devices and cupping. But at Limestone Therapeutics, a massage studio a few blocks from the Country Club Plaza in Kansas City, the treatment is back, and busier than it's been in years.

"We get clients who tell us they haven't had a hot stone massage since a spa day a decade ago, and they're shocked at how different it feels now," said studio founder Desmond Okonkwo, a licensed massage therapist who opened Limestone Therapeutics in 2021 after years working in sports rehabilitation clinics around the Kansas City metro.

## Updating a Classic Technique

The difference, Okonkwo said, is in how the treatment is structured. Rather than treating hot stones purely as a relaxation tool, Limestone's therapists incorporate them into a broader session that includes targeted deep tissue work on areas of chronic tension, informed by the same principles Okonkwo used when treating athletes.

"The heat does something deep tissue work alone can't," Okonkwo explained. "It increases blood flow and lets the muscle release faster, so we can work more effectively without having to use as much pressure. It's not just about feeling good in the moment. It's about actually changing how the tissue responds."

The studio's basalt stones are heated in a temperature-controlled unit and rotated throughout each session to maintain consistent warmth, a detail Okonkwo said separates a well-run hot stone treatment from a mediocre one. "If the stones cool down mid-session, the whole experience falls apart. We check temperature constantly."

## A Client Base That Skews Younger

Perhaps the most notable shift, according to therapist Bianca Reyes, who joined the studio last year, is who's booking the treatment. "It used to be thought of as something your mom did for a birthday treat," Reyes said. "Now I've got clients in their late twenties and thirties booking it regularly, especially people who work at desks all day near the Plaza and are dealing with neck and shoulder tension."

Reyes said many newer clients discover the treatment through word of mouth from friends who work in the offices and retail businesses surrounding the Plaza, a dense commercial corridor where desk-bound tension is a common complaint.

## Building Relationships With Local Physical Therapists

Limestone Therapeutics has also cultivated referral relationships with several physical therapy clinics in the Kansas City area, positioning the studio as a complement to rehabilitation work rather than a purely indulgent add-on. Okonkwo said roughly a quarter of new clients now arrive with a referral from a physical therapist managing a client's recovery from an injury or surgery.

"That relationship matters to us," Okonkwo said. "We're not trying to replace medical treatment. We're trying to be part of a broader plan for someone's recovery and long-term comfort."

## What's Next

Okonkwo said the studio plans to expand its therapist roster by two positions this year to keep pace with demand, and is considering adding a second treatment room specifically designed for the hot stone protocol, complete with dimmable lighting and a dedicated stone-warming station.

"There's something almost old-fashioned about it that people are craving again," Okonkwo said. "In a world where everything feels rushed and digital, lying down with warm stone against tense muscle is about as analog and human as it gets."
