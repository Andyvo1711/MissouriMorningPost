A customer of Sunny Creative Designs describes a nail service that ended with an injured finger and difficulty reaching the salon afterward. Their continued effort to get in touch is a meaningful signal, because a customer who keeps calling is still giving the team a chance to respond. This account highlights two areas that matter to every nail professional: careful handling of adhesive and a dependable way to follow up when something goes wrong.

## Glue Contact With the Skin

The customer says the underside of their finger was glued to the nail, which caused the skin to tear and the finger to bleed. Nail adhesive bonds quickly, so small habits make a large difference. A technician can apply only a small amount, keep the product on the nail surface, wipe away any excess before it sets, and check underneath the free edge before moving to the next finger. Asking the customer to keep their fingers relaxed and apart while the product cures also lowers the chance of skin touching the glue.

If skin does become bonded, the safest approach is to avoid pulling. Pausing the service, using an appropriate remover or warm soapy water as the product directions advise, and gently allowing the bond to release can help prevent further harm. If the skin breaks, the team should clean the area, cover it with a fresh dressing, explain what happened, and encourage the customer to seek medical care if bleeding continues or signs of irritation appear. Writing down the details right away helps everyone respond consistently.

## Reaching the Salon After the Visit

The customer also says they have been calling every day and are "always sent a voicemail." For someone dealing with an injury, silence can add worry to discomfort. Even when a team is busy with appointments, a simple system helps. One person can be assigned to check voicemail at set times each day, return health and safety concerns first, and note when each call was answered. A clear voicemail greeting that gives an expected callback window, along with a second contact option such as text or email, lets customers know their message will be seen.

When the call is returned, listening calmly, expressing concern for how the customer is feeling, and discussing next steps shows that the salon takes the matter seriously. Inviting the customer to speak with the owner or manager can also help resolve the concern directly.

## Editorial Perspective

Three lessons stand out from this case. First, handle nail glue with precision, using small amounts and checking the skin around and under each nail before the product sets. Second, if skin is bonded or injured, stop, avoid pulling, give basic first aid, and encourage medical care when needed. Third, return calls promptly, especially when a customer reports an injury, and keep a clear record of every contact. A timely, caring response protects the customer and gives the salon the best chance to rebuild trust.
