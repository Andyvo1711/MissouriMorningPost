A Missouri University of Science and Technology biochemist has received a $528,000 grant from the National Institutes of Health to study how iron acquisition contributes to infections by bacteria and fungi, the university announced Nov. 10.

## The target

Dr. Pablo Sobrado, the Richard K. Vitek/FCR Endowed Chair of Biochemistry, leads the work, which focuses on *Aspergillus fumigatus*, a common mold that can cause serious lung infections. The fungus produces siderophores, molecules that bind iron in the body. Aspergillosis is especially dangerous for immunocompromised patients. About 200,000 new human cases occur each year, and roughly half of those patients die even with antifungal treatment.

## Why it matters

Sobrado said the study aims to pave the way for a new class of antibiotics that can combat antibiotic-resistant pathogens, noting that antibiotic resistance renders commonly prescribed drugs increasingly ineffective.

## Training students

The project will also train undergraduates in protein chemistry, enzyme characterization, inhibitor design and validation, and structural biochemistry.
