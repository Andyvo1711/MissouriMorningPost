Branson's reputation has long rested on live music theaters, family attractions, and its stretch of Ozark lakefront, but a quieter tourism niche has been building steadily on the outskirts of town: multi-day wellness retreats drawing visitors from across the Midwest looking for something slower than a typical Branson trip.

Ozark Hollow Retreat, set on forty acres of wooded hillside about fifteen minutes from the main strip, has become one of the more established names in that growing niche since opening its retreat program four years ago. Its weekend packages, which combine guided hikes, yoga sessions, and a largely device-free schedule, have sold out consistently for the past three seasons.

## Reimagining What Branson Tourism Can Be

"People hear Branson and think theaters and go-karts, and that's a huge part of what makes this area special," said retreat director Hollis Vance, who moved to the area from Kansas City to launch the program. "But this region also has this incredible natural quiet that most visitors never really experience because they're moving between attractions all day."

Ozark Hollow's flagship offering, a three-night "Reset Weekend," includes twice-daily yoga sessions held in an open-air pavilion overlooking a ravine, a guided forest bathing walk led by a certified nature therapy guide, and communal meals built around produce sourced from small farms in Taney and Stone counties.

Vance said the retreat deliberately avoids scheduling anything resembling a typical vacation itinerary. "There's no rush here. We tell people on day one to just expect to feel a little bored by the second afternoon, and then something shifts. That's usually when people actually start to relax."

## A Broader Shift in Branson's Visitor Base

Local tourism officials have taken note of the trend. While Branson's core visitor base remains largely built around entertainment and family travel, wellness-focused offerings have expanded across the area in recent years, including yoga classes offered at several lakeside resorts and at least two other smaller retreat operators that have opened since Ozark Hollow's debut.

"We're seeing a segment of travelers who want the natural beauty of the Ozarks without the high-energy itinerary," said tourism consultant Marguerite Ellison, who has studied visitor trends in the region. "Branson's setting, with the lakes and the hills, is genuinely suited to that kind of travel. It just hasn't always been marketed that way."

## Guests Seeking a Genuine Reset

Guest reviews of Ozark Hollow frequently mention the retreat's no-phones-at-meals policy and its emphasis on unstructured downtime as standout features. Chicago-based guest Priyanka Dev, who attended a retreat weekend last year, said it was unlike any trip she'd taken to the region before.

"I'd been to Branson as a kid for the shows, but this felt like a completely different destination," Dev said. "Waking up to actual silence, no notifications, just birds and the sound of the creek nearby, that's not something I expected to find in Missouri."

## Expansion Plans

Vance said Ozark Hollow is adding a fourth retreat weekend to its 2026 fall calendar due to demand and is exploring a partnership with a local massage therapist to bring bodywork services on-site, rather than requiring guests to travel into town.

"There's room for both versions of Branson to thrive," Vance said. "The show tickets and the stillness. We're just trying to make sure people know the second option exists."
