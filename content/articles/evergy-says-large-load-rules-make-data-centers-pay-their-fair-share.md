Evergy told customers this week that data centers and other very large power users will pay their fair share under new "large load" rules, KCTV5 reported Oct. 26.

## What the rules require

The rules apply to any customer with peak demand above 75 megawatts, including data centers, large manufacturers and logistics centers. Those customers must:

- Pay higher rates than other large commercial and industrial customers.
- Pay 100% of infrastructure built solely to serve them, such as substations and transmission lines.
- Pay a minimum monthly bill and post collateral equal to two years of minimum bills.
- Sign contracts of at least 12 years, with an optional five-year phase-in.
- Face exit fees if they leave early.

## Missouri timeline

Missouri passed Senate Bill 4 in 2025, requiring the Public Service Commission to make sure large load customers pay their share without shifting costs to residential and small-business customers. Evergy's Missouri tariffs took effect in March; Ameren Missouri's took effect in January.

## The Independence gap

The rules cover only investor-owned utilities regulated by the Public Service Commission. Independence, which has a city-run utility, approved incentives for the Nebius data center, which will need more than 800 megawatts. Voters later ousted two council members and recalled another over those incentives.

An Evergy spokesperson said the rates were established so that current customers are protected while data centers pay their fair share.
