When Ironwood Reformer Co. opened near Springfield's Galloway neighborhood two years ago, owner Priscilla Doan wasn't sure the city had enough demand to support a studio built entirely around reformer Pilates, a discipline that had, until then, been concentrated mostly in Kansas City and St. Louis.

"I had people ask me why I wasn't just adding a few reformer machines to a regular gym," Doan said. "But that's not really how the format works best. You need trained instructors watching form closely, and you need machines that aren't constantly booked by someone doing something else."

## Betting on a Dedicated Space

Doan, a former competitive gymnast who discovered Pilates during a shoulder injury recovery, opened Ironwood with twelve reformer machines and a small but highly trained instructor staff, all certified through a comprehensive program that requires roughly 450 hours of training before instructors can lead a class independently.

The bet paid off faster than she expected. Within its first year, Ironwood had built a waitlist for its most popular class times, particularly early morning sessions favored by Springfield professionals looking to fit a workout in before work. The studio has since expanded its class schedule from four to nine sessions a day.

"There wasn't really a dedicated option like this in Springfield before," said client Renata Osei, a regular at the studio since it opened. "I used to drive up to Kansas City occasionally for a proper reformer class. Now I don't have to."

## Building a Teaching Culture, Not Just a Class Schedule

Doan has been deliberate about instructor development, requiring ongoing continuing education and pairing newer teachers with mentors for their first several months leading classes independently. She said this attention to instruction quality, more than the equipment itself, is what keeps clients returning.

"Anyone can buy a reformer machine," Doan said. "What's harder to build is a team of instructors who can actually see when someone's alignment is off from across the room and correct it before it becomes an injury."

Instructor Callum Bright, who joined Ironwood after relocating to Springfield from Colorado, said the studio's emphasis on small class sizes, capped at eight participants, sets it apart from larger fitness chains that have started adding Pilates-inspired classes to their own schedules. "You actually get seen here," Bright said. "I know my regulars' injury histories, their goals, what they're working around. That's not really possible in a class of thirty."

## Serving a Broader Range of Bodies

The studio has also built a specific track for clients recovering from injury or managing chronic conditions, working in coordination with local physical therapists. Doan said roughly fifteen percent of Ironwood's clients were referred by a physical therapist or chiropractor in the Springfield area.

"Reformer Pilates is low-impact enough that it works for a huge range of people, from athletes cross-training to someone rebuilding strength after surgery," Doan said. "We wanted the studio to reflect that range, not just cater to people who are already extremely fit."

## Growth on the Horizon

With demand still outpacing available class slots, Doan said Ironwood is exploring a second location on the north side of Springfield, tentatively planned for later this year. "This city was ready for this," she said. "We just had to be the ones to bring it here."
