Americans over 50 with entrepreneurial experience had about a 10% lower risk of death than those in traditional jobs, according to a study co-authored by a Missouri S&T researcher and published in the *Journal of Business Venturing*. S&T reported the findings Oct. 30.

## The research

Dr. Hanqing Fang, an associate professor in S&T's Dennis and Janet Jaggi School of Business, and colleagues analyzed data from more than 27,500 Americans over age 50.

## Why you became an entrepreneur matters

Owning a business is likely not the deciding factor. The advantage is concentrated among people who chose entrepreneurship when they had other options. Benefits such as control, freedom and ownership are harder to achieve when financial pressure or limited alternatives drive the decision. Current entrepreneurs showed better physical health, while the mental health advantage was clearest among people whose entrepreneurial experience was in the past.

## Takeaways

Fang said the question is not whether to become an entrepreneur but why. He suggested employers could build similar benefits into traditional jobs by giving employees more autonomy, and compared the lifespan benefit to exercise, where persistence matters.
