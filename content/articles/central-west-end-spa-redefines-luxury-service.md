On a quiet stretch of Maryland Avenue in the Central West End, past a row of century-old brick facades, a heavy oak door opens onto a lobby scented faintly with cedar and bergamot. This is Aurelia Bath House, a twelve-room spa that opened eighteen months ago and has since become something of an open secret among St. Louis residents who want a spa experience that feels less like a transaction and more like an occasion.

Owner and lead esthetician Odette Marchand spent nearly a decade working in spas in Chicago and Scottsdale before returning to her home state to open her own operation. "I wanted to build the kind of place where the service matches the setting," Marchand said, gesturing toward the hydrotherapy room, where a copper soaking tub sits beneath a skylight salvaged from the building's original 1908 construction. "St. Louis has incredible bones, architecturally. I didn't want to cover any of that up with something generic."

## A Menu Built Around Ritual, Not Just Treatment

What distinguishes Aurelia from the dozen or so day spas scattered across the Central West End and neighboring Forest Park Southeast isn't just the interior design. It's the pacing. Every service, from the ninety-minute "Gateway Facial" to the four-hand massage performed by two therapists working in tandem, begins with a ten-minute tea service in a small library room stocked with botanical texts and a fireplace that runs even in July, thanks to a ventless gel insert.

"Clients tell us they feel like they're being welcomed somewhere, not just checked in," said Priya Nandakumar, the spa's massage therapy director, who trained in Ayurvedic bodywork before relocating to Missouri three years ago. "We slow everything down on purpose. That's the whole point."

The spa's signature treatment, a ninety-minute ritual called the Forest Park Restoration, combines a dry brush exfoliation, a magnesium-infused wrap, and a scalp massage performed with warmed oil. It runs $285 and has a waitlist most Saturdays.

## Staying Rooted in the Neighborhood

Despite the elevated price point, Marchand has resisted pressure to expand into a second location downtown or in Clayton, at least for now. "This neighborhood raised the kind of clientele who appreciate craft," she said. "I'd rather get better at one location than dilute what we've built by spreading thin."

That philosophy extends to staffing. Aurelia employs eleven full-time estheticians and massage therapists, all of whom receive continuing education stipends and are encouraged to develop their own signature treatments. Nandakumar's four-hand massage technique, developed specifically for the spa, has become one of its most requested bookings, often reserved six weeks in advance around Valentine's Day and Mother's Day.

Local hospitality consultant Ben Ferris, who has advised several St. Louis wellness businesses, said Aurelia represents a broader shift in how the city's luxury service sector is positioning itself. "For a long time, if you wanted that level of spa experience, you drove to Chicago or flew somewhere," Ferris said. "What's happening in the Central West End now is a signal that St. Louis clients don't want to leave the city to feel pampered anymore."

## Looking Ahead

Marchand said she's exploring a partnership with a local herbalist to develop a seasonal line of body oils exclusive to the spa, with a projected launch later this year. For now, she's focused on maintaining the unhurried atmosphere that has become Aurelia's calling card.

"People come here stressed," she said. "My job is to make sure they leave feeling like they actually took a breath for the first time all week."
