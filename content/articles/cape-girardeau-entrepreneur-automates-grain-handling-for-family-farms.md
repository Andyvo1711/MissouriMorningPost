Adelina Prosser-Kimathi grew up helping her family haul grain by hand on a farm outside Cape Girardeau, work she says gave her a lasting appreciation for how physically punishing grain handling remains for small and mid-sized operations. That appreciation eventually became River Bend Grain Robotics, a company she founded in 2019 to build automated grain handling systems priced for farms that can't afford the massive equipment packages sold to industrial-scale operations.

"The big automation companies build for farms with ten thousand acres," Prosser-Kimathi said at the company's assembly facility on the outskirts of Cape Girardeau. "Most Missouri farms aren't that. I wanted to build for the farm my family actually had."

## Designing Down, Not Just Building Up

River Bend Grain Robotics's flagship product is a modular conveyor and monitoring system that automates grain movement between storage bins and transport vehicles, a process traditionally requiring significant manual labor during harvest season. Prosser-Kimathi said the engineering challenge wasn't building something that worked, but building something that worked at a price point family farms could actually justify.

"Anyone can build an expensive solution," she said. "Building a genuinely useful system for under a third of what the big manufacturers charge took years of stripping out everything that wasn't essential."

The company's approach has resonated in the region. River Bend Grain Robotics now has installations on more than 300 farms across southeast Missouri and neighboring parts of Illinois and Kentucky, with a waiting list for installation before the fall harvest season each year.

## Financing Innovation on a Farmer's Budget

Prosser-Kimathi has structured much of the company's sales around flexible financing arrangements tailored to agricultural cash flow cycles, allowing farmers to pay for systems after harvest rather than upfront. That decision, she said, came directly from her own family's experience with equipment purchases that assumed a level of cash liquidity most family farms don't have during the growing season.

"Farm equipment financing is usually built around what works for the bank, not what works for the farm," she said. "We built ours around when farmers actually have money."

## Growing Without Losing Focus

River Bend Grain Robotics now employs 47 people, including a growing service team that handles installation and seasonal maintenance across its multi-state footprint. Prosser-Kimathi has resisted pressure from investors to expand into unrelated agricultural technology categories, insisting the company stay focused on grain handling until it has fully saturated its core market.

The company is currently developing a smaller-scale version of its conveyor system aimed at farms under 500 acres, a segment Prosser-Kimathi believes has been almost entirely ignored by the broader agricultural automation industry. "There are thousands of farms that size in this state alone," she said. "Somebody should be building for them, and I'd rather it be us than nobody."
