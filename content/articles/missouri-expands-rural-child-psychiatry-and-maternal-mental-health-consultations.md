Missouri is expanding behavioral health consultations for children, families, and pregnant and postpartum patients through the federal Rural Health Transformation Program, the Department of Social Services announced Sept. 22.

## Two programs, up to $6.4 million

The funding, up to $6.4 million combined, expands two programs run in partnership with the Department of Mental Health, the Department of Health and Senior Services' Office on Women's Health and the University of Missouri's Center for Child Well-Being.

- **Missouri Child Psychiatry Access Project (MO-CPAP):** connects pediatricians, family medicine providers, school personnel and other front-line professionals with child and adolescent psychiatrists for provider-to-provider teleconsultation, typically within 30 minutes, plus referral services and training.
- **Missouri Maternal Health Access Project (MO-MHAP):** supports OB-GYNs, midwives and other perinatal providers with psychiatric consultation and care coordination, and adds cardiometabolic care through Maternal-Fetal Medicine specialists.

## Why it matters

The state says cardiometabolic conditions are a leading cause of maternal mortality in Missouri. The programs also increase provider availability, including outside standard business hours, and offer training for the rural maternal health workforce.

*Source: Missouri Department of Social Services bulletin (Sept. 22, 2026).*
