Missouri Southern State University in Joplin has expanded partnerships with regional manufacturers and employers, part of an effort to align academic programs more closely with the specific workforce needs of southwest Missouri's industrial base.

The university sits in a region with a substantial manufacturing and logistics presence, and officials say the new partnerships are designed to ensure graduates leave with skills that translate directly into available local jobs, rather than relying solely on broad degree credentials that may not map cleanly onto employer expectations. Several regional manufacturers have agreed to offer expanded internship and apprenticeship opportunities tied to specific academic programs, including engineering technology and business management.

## Aligning Curriculum With Regional Industry

"We're in a part of the state where manufacturing and logistics are huge employers, and we wanted our programs to reflect that reality more directly," said a Missouri Southern workforce development administrator. "That means talking regularly with local employers about what skills gaps they're actually seeing, rather than assuming we already know."

As part of the partnership expansion, the university has adjusted portions of its engineering technology curriculum based on direct feedback from manufacturing partners, incorporating more coursework on automation systems and quality control processes that employers said were increasingly relevant to entry-level technical roles. University officials describe this kind of curriculum responsiveness as a key advantage of a regional university closely tied to its local economy.

The partnerships also include new scholarship funding from participating employers, some of which is tied to students committing to complete an internship or entry-level position with the sponsoring company after graduation, an arrangement designed to benefit both students seeking financial support and employers hoping to secure a reliable talent pipeline.

## Addressing Local Talent Retention

Regional economic development officials say the partnership expansion addresses a persistent concern in southwest Missouri: retaining college-educated workers in the area rather than losing them to larger metro job markets like Kansas City, St. Louis, or out of state entirely. By building direct pathways from campus to local employers, university officials hope to increase the share of graduates who choose to build careers in the Joplin area.

Students participating in the expanded internship programs say the experience has helped clarify their career plans well before graduation. "I had a general idea of what I wanted to do, but the internship gave me an actual sense of the day-to-day work," said a senior studying industrial technology who completed a semester-long placement with a regional manufacturer. "It also made the job search a lot less intimidating, since I already had a foot in the door."

University leaders say they plan to continue expanding these employer partnerships across additional academic departments, including logistics and supply chain management, an area they describe as a growing part of the regional economy. Officials note that sustaining these partnerships requires ongoing coordination, since curriculum changes typically move more slowly than shifts in employer needs, but describe the current collaborative approach as a meaningful improvement over more static, employer-disconnected program design.
