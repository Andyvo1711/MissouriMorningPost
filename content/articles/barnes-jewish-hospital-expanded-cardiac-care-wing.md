A steady stream of visitors moved through the newly expanded cardiac care wing at Barnes-Jewish Hospital last week, taking in wider hallways, additional catheterization labs, and a redesigned recovery floor built to handle a growing number of heart patients from across the St. Louis region. The expansion, part of BJC HealthCare's broader investment in cardiovascular services, adds dozens of monitored beds and two new procedure suites to a campus that already ranks among the busiest heart care centers in Missouri.

Hospital administrators say the project responds to years of rising demand, as an aging population and improved survival rates for heart attack and heart failure patients have pushed occupancy in the existing cardiac unit well above comfortable levels. "We were seeing patients boarded in hallways during peak admission periods, which is not the standard of care we want to deliver," said a BJC HealthCare spokesperson familiar with the project. "This expansion gives our clinical teams room to work and gives patients a calmer place to recover."

## A Regional Draw for Complex Cases

Barnes-Jewish has long served as a referral center for complicated cardiac cases transferred from smaller hospitals across Missouri and southern Illinois, including patients needing minimally invasive valve replacements or mechanical circulatory support. The new wing includes a dedicated transfer bay intended to speed the handoff of patients arriving by ambulance or helicopter from rural facilities that lack advanced cardiac capabilities.

Nurse leaders on the unit describe the redesign as much about workflow as square footage. Supply rooms sit closer to patient bays, medication stations are positioned to reduce walking distance during emergencies, and each room includes space for family members to stay overnight, a change requested repeatedly in patient satisfaction surveys. "Families want to be present, and we wanted the physical space to support that instead of fighting against it," said a nurse practitioner who works on the cardiac step-down floor.

## Addressing a Statewide Pattern

The investment arrives as cardiovascular disease remains one of the leading causes of death in Missouri, with rural counties often reporting higher rates of hospitalization for heart-related conditions than the St. Louis metro area itself. Public health researchers point to a combination of factors, including limited access to preventive cardiology, higher rates of smoking and diabetes in some rural communities, and longer transport times to specialized care.

BJC officials say the expanded wing is intended to complement, not replace, efforts to strengthen cardiac care closer to home for rural Missourians. The health system has continued to expand outreach cardiology clinics in smaller towns, staffed on a rotating basis by specialists who otherwise practice in St. Louis, with the goal of catching problems earlier and reducing the number of patients who need emergency transfer in the first place.

## What Comes Next

Construction crews will spend the coming months finishing a connected outpatient testing center adjacent to the new wing, which will house stress testing, echocardiography, and cardiac rehabilitation services under one roof. Hospital planners say consolidating these services is meant to reduce the number of separate appointments patients must schedule after a cardiac event, a logistical burden that can be especially difficult for those traveling in from outside the metro area.

For patients like those recovering on the unit this month, the changes are felt in smaller ways: quieter rooms, easier parking near the new entrance, and staff who say they finally have enough space to do their jobs without constant improvisation. Hospital leaders acknowledge the work is not finished, noting that continued growth in cardiac admissions will likely require further planning within the decade, but for now they describe the expansion as a meaningful step toward keeping pace with the needs of a growing patient population across eastern Missouri.
