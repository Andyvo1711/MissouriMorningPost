Small business lending activity has picked up noticeably across many of Missouri's smaller cities and towns, according to community bankers who describe renewed entrepreneurial interest in downtown storefronts that had sat vacant or underutilized for years. From coffee shops and boutique retailers to specialty contractors and professional service firms, small business owners across the state are seeking financing to open, expand, or take over existing operations.

The trend appears to be strongest in towns with active Main Street revitalization programs, where local governments and business associations have worked to make historic downtown districts more attractive for new business formation. Several Missouri communities have used a combination of facade improvement grants, reduced permitting fees, and marketing support to draw entrepreneurs back to downtown cores that had lost retail activity to suburban shopping centers over previous decades.

## Community Banks as the Primary Financing Source

For most small business owners in smaller Missouri markets, community banks remain the primary source of financing, offering the kind of relationship-based underwriting that larger national banks are often unable or unwilling to provide for smaller loan amounts. Community bank executives say they continue to prioritize small business lending as a core part of their mission, even as loan sizes in this segment tend to be less profitable on a per-transaction basis than larger commercial deals.

A community bank lending officer who focuses on small business clients said federal loan guarantee programs remain an important tool for many of these transactions, helping offset risk for lenders financing new businesses without long operating histories. Loan officers report that first-time business owners, in particular, rely heavily on these guarantee programs to secure financing they otherwise might not qualify for through conventional underwriting alone.

## Credit Unions Expanding Their Role

Credit unions, traditionally more focused on consumer lending, have also become more active in small business financing across Missouri in recent years, particularly in rural and small-town markets where banking competition is limited. Several credit unions have added dedicated business lending staff and expanded their commercial loan products to meet growing member demand.

Credit union leaders say this expansion reflects both member requests and a broader strategic push to diversify revenue beyond traditional consumer products like auto loans and mortgages, which have faced more competitive pressure from online lenders in recent years.

## Challenges Facing New Business Owners

Despite the positive lending trends, small business owners across the state continue to cite rising costs, particularly for commercial insurance, construction materials, and labor, as significant challenges to opening or expanding a business. Interest rates on small business loans, while more favorable than at their recent peak, remain elevated relative to the historically low rates business owners enjoyed in the previous decade, affecting the math on expansion decisions.

Local economic development officials say they have tried to address some of these cost pressures through targeted grant programs, though funding availability varies significantly from one Missouri community to the next based on local budget priorities and state grant allocations.

## A Cautiously Encouraging Trend

Taken together, the pickup in small business lending activity across Missouri's smaller markets represents a meaningful, if uneven, bright spot in the state's economic picture. Community bankers describe the trend as sustainable rather than a temporary blip, driven by genuine entrepreneurial interest in underserved downtown markets rather than speculative activity. Whether that momentum continues will likely depend on broader economic conditions, but for now, Main Streets across Missouri are seeing a level of small business investment that many communities have not experienced in years.
