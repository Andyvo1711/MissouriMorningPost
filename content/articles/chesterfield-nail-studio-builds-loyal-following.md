Tucked into a strip center off Chesterfield Airport Road, not far from the Chesterfield Valley shopping corridor, Gilded Petal Nail Studio doesn't look like it's trying to compete on volume. There are only six manicure stations, each spaced generously apart, and the studio caps its daily bookings well below what most of its neighbors along the retail strip attempt to squeeze into a shift.

That's by design, according to owner Reina Castellanos, who opened the studio four years ago after working as a technician at larger salons in both Chesterfield and St. Louis County. "I used to do twelve, thirteen sets a day at my old job," Castellanos said. "Your hands hurt, your focus slips, and honestly the client doesn't get your best work by appointment number ten. I wanted to build something different."

## A Different Business Model

Gilded Petal caps each technician at seven appointments per day, a deliberate departure from the industry standard in the area. Castellanos said the studio makes up the difference in revenue by charging slightly more than competitors and by retaining clients longer, since fewer people report chipping, lifting, or uneven application, the most common complaints that send customers salon-hopping.

The studio has also invested heavily in ongoing education. Every technician at Gilded Petal, including Castellanos herself, attends at least two out-of-state training intensives per year, covering everything from advanced gel-X extensions to hand-painted nail art techniques that have become the studio's signature.

"Reina brought us all to a certification course in Nashville last spring," said technician Yuki Okafor, who joined the studio two years ago after relocating to Chesterfield from Kansas City. "Most salons don't invest in that. It made me feel like this was a place I could actually build a career, not just clock hours."

## Health and Safety as a Selling Point

Castellanos has also made sanitation practices a centerpiece of the studio's marketing, a response to broader industry concerns about improperly sterilized tools and inadequate ventilation in nail salons. Gilded Petal uses hospital-grade autoclave sterilization for all metal implements and installed an enhanced ventilation system above each station when it opened, exceeding what Missouri state cosmetology regulations require.

"A lot of clients don't ask about it, but the ones who do really appreciate that we can explain our process in detail," Castellanos said. "It's not flashy, but it's the kind of thing that keeps people coming back for years instead of months."

## Community Roots in Chesterfield Valley

The studio has also become something of a fixture for local small-business collaboration. Gilded Petal regularly partners with a nearby bridal boutique to offer package deals for wedding parties, and Castellanos hosts a monthly evening event where a portion of proceeds from nail art bookings goes to a rotating local charity, a practice she started during the studio's first year and has maintained even as the business has grown busier.

Client Marla Whitfield, a Chesterfield resident who has been coming to the studio since it opened, said the atmosphere is part of the draw. "It doesn't feel rushed," Whitfield said. "I've been to places where you can tell they're trying to get you out the door. Here, they actually talk to you, and the work lasts twice as long."

## Looking to the Future

Castellanos said she has no immediate plans to open a second location, despite regular inquiries from clients hoping for a studio closer to Wildwood or Ballwin. "I'd rather keep this one running exactly the way I want it," she said. "Growth for growth's sake isn't really the goal. Doing right by the people already walking through the door is."
