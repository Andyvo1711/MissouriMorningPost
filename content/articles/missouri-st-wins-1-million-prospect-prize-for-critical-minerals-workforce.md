Missouri University of Science and Technology is one of 16 universities selected by the U.S. Department of Energy to receive a $1 million PROSPECT Planning Prize, the university announced Oct. 26. The goal of the program is to quickly grow the critical minerals and materials workforce.

## The proposal

S&T's winning proposal, "CM3: Critical Minerals, Metals and Manufacturing," aims to draw students from materials science, geological, civil, chemical, mechanical and environmental engineering and business, not just mining. Existing courses will be combined into stackable, industry-targeted certificates and rapid workforce credentials.

The effort is led by Dr. Catherine Johnson, interim chair of mining and explosives engineering, with Dr. Stephen Gao and Dr. Sridhar Seetharaman.

## Facilities behind the plan

The program is anchored by S&T's $38 million Tech Hub pilot-scale processing test bed, the $16.3 million DOE Mine of the Future proving ground, the Experimental Mine, the O'Keefe Center for Critical Minerals, the Robert V. Wolf Foundry and the Missouri Protoplex.

## Why it matters

DOE Assistant Secretary Audrey Robertson said America has abundant natural resources but needs the skilled workforce, and that PROSPECT aims to reverse a decline in the mining and critical minerals workforce. Johnson said the prize shows mining's role in national security, advanced manufacturing and energy technologies.
