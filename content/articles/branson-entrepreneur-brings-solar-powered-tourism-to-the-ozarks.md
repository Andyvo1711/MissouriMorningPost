Winifred Castellane spotted an opportunity that most renewable energy companies overlooked when she founded Table Rock Renewables in 2019: Branson's massive concentration of hotels, theaters, and attractions represented some of the highest commercial energy users in southwest Missouri, yet almost none of them had explored solar power as a way to control costs. Six years later, her company has installed solar systems on more than 50 commercial properties across the Branson tourism corridor.

"Everybody thinks about solar for homes first," Castellane said, inspecting a rooftop installation atop a Branson entertainment venue. "But a theater running matinee and evening shows six days a week has an energy bill that would make most homeowners faint. That's where the real savings are."

## Pitching Sustainability as a Business Case

Castellane's sales approach deliberately avoids leading with environmental arguments, instead framing solar installation as a straightforward cost-reduction strategy for tourism businesses operating on tight seasonal margins. She said this pragmatic pitch has proven far more effective with Branson business owners than appeals centered on sustainability alone.

"I don't need a theater owner to care about climate change to sell them solar," Castellane said. "I need them to see the electric bill from last August and understand there's a better way to handle that number."

That approach has helped Table Rock Renewables build a client base that includes several of Branson's mid-sized hotels and at least two entertainment venues, with commercial installations that typically pay for themselves within five to seven years through reduced utility costs.

## Navigating a Seasonal Installation Schedule

Branson's tourism economy creates a unique operational challenge for Table Rock Renewables, since most commercial clients want installation work completed during the region's slower winter season to avoid disrupting peak-season operations. Castellane said this seasonal compression forces the company to complete the bulk of its annual installation work in a concentrated few months, requiring careful crew scheduling and equipment planning.

"We do in four months what a lot of solar companies get to spread across a year," she said. "It's intense, but it's also predictable, and predictable is something you can plan a business around."

## Expanding Beyond Commercial Rooftops

Table Rock Renewables now employs 41 people, including a growing engineering team that designs custom systems for the irregular rooflines common among Branson's entertainment venues. Castellane has recently begun exploring ground-mounted solar arrays for attractions with sufficient surrounding land, a project type that could open a new segment of the local tourism market to the company's services.

She is also working with a regional tourism association to develop a certification program recognizing Branson businesses that adopt renewable energy, an initiative she believes could help solar installation become a marketing advantage rather than simply a cost-saving measure. "Ozark tourism sells itself on natural beauty," Castellane said. "It only makes sense that the businesses selling that experience should be powered a little more naturally too."
